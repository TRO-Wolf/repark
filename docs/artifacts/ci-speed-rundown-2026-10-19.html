The flakes: three cards, three different answers

Five re-runs were needed during the chain: the OCC storm twice, the fence pin once, the spill pin once, and one facade shard that hung past fifty minutes and was cancelled. You authorised two Opus 5.5 executors on the first two. Each ran a measured step 0 before touching anything.

MB-FENCE-BODY-FLAKE-1 fixed · #1108

Verdict: product gap, not a pin over-asserting. Run::enter_scope loaded the sink before entering the process-wide batch scope, so a second driver could run a foreachBatch body for an epoch the first driver had just committed. That breaks MB-3 clause D-19.

Fix. Enter the scope under the registered sink uuid first, load the sink under it, and re-enter if the uuid changed (a replaced sink). Deterministic pin a_winner_that_commits_during_the_batch_load_never_lets_a_fenced_body_run was red 4 of 4 on main and is green after the fix; a second pin covers the replaced-sink path. The existing pin is unchanged.

Replays. 209 timing replays never reproduced the fenced body. Every replay failure was the PA-15 10-second bound, traced to fsync on this box (p99 0.82 s, max 1.76 s). Card: task/roadmap/mid-term/mb-fence-body-flake-1-card-2026-10-18.md; ledger mb-fence-body-flake-1-ledger.md, C-001 to C-007.

OCC-DISJOINT-MERGE-FLAKE-1 legal · ruling needed · #1109

Verdict: no product race. 990 natural storms gave exactly one winner of eight every time. Holding one thread back 300 ms gives two of eight in 75 of 80 runs, and live Spark 4.1.2 gives two of eight in 40 of 40 with a 3-second late starter. The second winner is a legal serial history: it loaded after the first commit and validated against the new snapshot (on v3 it rewrites the first winner's delete vector). The fork's validation is correct as read.

Why the DataFrame door only. GIL work before the table load delays one thread; a forced GC makes the SQL door do the same. The CI stall source is inferred, not observed: no CI trace shows it, and the assertion message does not print the v3 summary that would witness it.

Pin unchanged. Ledger clause C-006 is OPEN for you; see decision 1 below. Card: occ-disjoint-merge-flake-1-card-2026-10-14.md, dated section added.

Decisions that are yours

1 · C-006: the storm pin's tolerance

The pin asserts exactly one winner of eight on every run. Options, with costs:

  1. Keep exact 1 of 8 and accept an occasional CI re-run (four sightings in twelve days).
  2. Gate the storm deterministically: a source UDF waits on a shared barrier after each MERGE's base load, like spark_occ_oracle3. Needs a new Spark recording with the same gate, and a check that eight blocked UDFs cannot starve the runtime's workers. The executor's lean and mine.
  3. Admit a proven-serial second winner, witnessed by the v3 removed-dvs summary. v3 only; v2 has no witness.
  4. Run the storm on the SQL door only.

Recommendation: option 2 as a follow-on clerk unit; option 1 until it lands.

2 · PA-15: the 10-second bound on a slow disk

The two-driver pin and its lifecycle sibling miss the ended bound when the warehouse disk is slow; measured to fsync on this host. The rules forbid raising the bound or serialising. Either record this measurement as PA-15's classification and keep the bound, or rule for a tmpfs warehouse in the fixture, which changes what the pins measure.

Recommendation: keep the bound, record the classification on the card. CI's disk has not shown this; the fence pin's one CI failure was the real bug.

Mechanics learned, so the next chain is faster

  • Strict protection means one CI round per merge. Seven PRs took 3.5 hours end to end; a merge queue would remove that but likely needs an organisation (not verified). The chain script lives at scratchpad/cispeed/merge/chain3.sh in the session scratchpad: update-branch API, wait, squash with the commit messages as the body, stop on any failed check.
  • GitHub's update-branch returns 422 on conflicts that git merges cleanly locally (#1105 after #1106, #1109 after #1108). A local merge of main pushed to the branch is the fix; force-push and git branch -f are denied in this session's auto mode anyway.
  • A cache key that exists is never re-saved. Changing what a warm job produces needs a new key prefix, which is why #1106 went to v3.
  • A hung facade shard runs for six hours. There is no timeout-minutes on the shards; one hung past fifty minutes on #1106 and was cancelled by hand.
  • Every flake was a finding. One product bug, one legal ordering with a tolerance question, one still open. None was retried into silence.
